## 1. Executive summary

The first Phase 5 decision must be the **estimand**, not the geometry library. “River width” can mean at least three different quantities at the same transect:

1. the width of one explicitly identified branch;
2. total wetted width, the sum of all separate wet intervals;
3. channel-belt span, the distance from the first outer wet bank to the last, including islands and dry bars in between.

They are not interchangeable. braidedSP most closely implements a branch-conditioned **sum of transect–concave-hull intersection lengths**. That is a geometric proxy for total wetted width inside the selected corridor—not proof of it—because its centerline buffer and hull can capture a neighbor, bridge dry gaps, or omit sparse water, and the packaged result does not retain the component intervals. RiverObs implements **inundated pixel area divided by node length**, an area-equivalent width assigned to a prior-database reach; it is neither a literal transect intersection nor automatically an individual anabranch width. swot_for_flood extracts raster flood extent and does not calculate river width. rulesofriveravulsion visualizes manually interpreted ICESat-2/FABDEM cross sections and uses a pre-existing `width_m` field, but does not derive SWOT or cross-section width in the audited code.

The external work supports several concepts worth evaluating: retain wet components rather than just one scalar; separate water-extent classes from WSE classes; use pixel area and `water_frac` for an area-equivalent estimator; make centerline/branch association explicit; and test sensitivity against independent imagery. It also reveals failure modes that must not become silent defaults: largest-component deletion, hole filling that erases islands, adaptive buffers without a neighboring-branch exclusion guard, quality-word omission, coordinate deduplication, and use of min/max point spread as a bank width.

SWOT PIXC Lab already supersedes important ingestion and auditability aspects of the reviewed research code: Version D discovery, exact boundary-inclusive AOI clipping, lossless multi-tile concatenation, per-pixel granule/tile provenance, metadata-driven quality-bit decoding, immutable raw data, explicit QC reason masks, and user-defined metric transect sampling. Phase 5 should build on those representations. It should not replace them with a reduced GeoDataFrame/raster that loses source attributes or duplicate pixels.

No reviewed source establishes a validated, generally applicable multi-channel-width algorithm for the Koshi. braidedSP provides the closest experimental template, but its reported low-flow PIXC width exceeded its Sentinel-2 width by about 196 m on average, and its authors omitted the high-flow width comparison because adaptive expansion could capture adjacent channels ([paper](https://doi.org/10.1029/2025GL116488), [stored analysis](https://github.com/selfr-eo/braidedSP/blob/84bfbd9232d3d69485b7f445c81e709ceacc3b3a/publication/notebooks/width_analysis.ipynb?plain=1#L1329-L1362)). Three architectures are therefore presented in Section 13 without selecting one.

## 3. Detailed review of each project

### 3.1 braidedSP

#### Inputs and water definition

**Paper.** The Brahmaputra case uses SWOT L2 HR PIXC Version C, pass 258 and tile 112L, on 2024-02-03 and 2024-07-19. PGC0 was preferred when available and PIC0 otherwise. Sentinel-2 L2A imagery was reduced to monthly median composites after rejecting scenes with more than 30% cloud and applying the 60 m scene-classification cloud mask. NDWI greater than 0.1 defines optical water; that threshold and the morphology parameters were chosen visually/empirically. September imagery substitutes for cloudy July conditions ([paper](https://doi.org/10.1029/2025GL116488)). The README's reproducible example names `SWOT_L2_HR_PIXC_010_258_112L_20240203T045957_20240203T050008_PIC0_01.nc` and `SWOT_L2_HR_PIXC_018_258_112L_20240719T030033_20240719T030044_PIC0_01.nc` ([README](https://github.com/selfr-eo/braidedSP/blob/84bfbd9232d3d69485b7f445c81e709ceacc3b3a/README.md#L51-L62)). The loader itself accepts a local NetCDF path and does not enforce a product version or CRID ([`_readPIXC`](https://github.com/selfr-eo/braidedSP/blob/84bfbd9232d3d69485b7f445c81e709ceacc3b3a/braidedSP/swot.py#L89-L116)).

The publication's exact centerline-mask settings are: 100 m initial dilation; Gaussian scale 200 m at low flow and 300 m at high flow; threshold 0.6; a second 100 m dilation; straight-line closure of skeleton endpoints less than 300 m apart; retention of mask holes only when they exceed the area of a circle of 3 km radius; iterative pruning of branches shorter than 6 km; and merging segments shorter than 2 km only when directional cosine similarity exceeds 0.7 ([paper](https://doi.org/10.1029/2025GL116488)). These are empirical, site-specific publication settings. They are not the same units/defaults as the current package's pixel-valued [`Mask.process`](https://github.com/selfr-eo/braidedSP/blob/84bfbd9232d3d69485b7f445c81e709ceacc3b3a/braidedSP/mask.py#L55-L100) arguments.

**Code.** The package takes already-local PIXC and binary mask files. `River.add_mask`, `process_masks`, `generate_centerlines`, `add_swot`, `process_swot`, and `extract_braidedSP` coordinate them by list order, not observation identifiers or dates ([`river.py::River`](https://github.com/selfr-eo/braidedSP/blob/84bfbd9232d3d69485b7f445c81e709ceacc3b3a/braidedSP/river.py#L17-L135)). The example additionally requires an AOI, manually simplified main centerline, and starting line ([notebook inputs](https://github.com/selfr-eo/braidedSP/blob/84bfbd9232d3d69485b7f445c81e709ceacc3b3a/examples/braidedSP_workflow.ipynb?plain=1#L12-L35)). SWORD v16 and official RiverSP appear in publication comparisons, not as required generation inputs. There is no DEM or ICESat-2 processing.

`_readPIXC` reads `classification`, coordinates, `height`, four correction fields, `water_frac`, `phase_noise_std`, `dheight_dphase`, and `sig0`, then reduces them to a GeoDataFrame ([source](https://github.com/selfr-eo/braidedSP/blob/84bfbd9232d3d69485b7f445c81e709ceacc3b3a/braidedSP/swot.py#L89-L143)). It does not retain original variable attributes/fill encodings, pixel area, per-pixel granule provenance, or PIXC quality words. `_translateClass` maps 3=`water_near_land`, 4=`open_water`, 5=`dark_water`, 6=`low_coh_water_near_land`, and 7=`open_low_coh_water`; `_readPIXC` keeps the exact caller-selected numeric values through `np.isin`. Package default/example keeps class 4; the paper-generation notebook requests classes 3 and 4 ([publication call](https://github.com/selfr-eo/braidedSP/blob/84bfbd9232d3d69485b7f445c81e709ceacc3b3a/publication/notebooks/gen_BraidedSP.ipynb?plain=1#L311-L312)). Classes 6/7 are named in code but not evaluated in the stored publication workflow. `water_frac` is summarized later but does not weight or define width.

The package is version `0.1.0` and declares Python `>=3.9` plus a broad geospatial/scientific stack ([`pyproject.toml`](https://github.com/selfr-eo/braidedSP/blob/84bfbd9232d3d69485b7f445c81e709ceacc3b3a/pyproject.toml)). Its core modules are `river.py`, `mask.py`, `centerline.py`, `swot.py`, and `tools.py`; publication-specific helpers and settings remain in notebooks. This audit found no tracked tests or CI in the frozen [repository tree](https://github.com/selfr-eo/braidedSP/tree/84bfbd9232d3d69485b7f445c81e709ceacc3b3a), and `.gitignore` excludes the PIXC, masks, and generated outputs needed to rerun the stored notebook results ([`.gitignore`](https://github.com/selfr-eo/braidedSP/blob/84bfbd9232d3d69485b7f445c81e709ceacc3b3a/.gitignore#L181-L184)). Engine choices are not fully mirrored by the declared dependency: `_readPIXC` defaults to `h5netcdf`, `River.process_swot` passes `netcdf`, and `pyproject.toml` directly declares `netcdf4` ([reader](https://github.com/selfr-eo/braidedSP/blob/84bfbd9232d3d69485b7f445c81e709ceacc3b3a/braidedSP/swot.py#L45-L91), [orchestration](https://github.com/selfr-eo/braidedSP/blob/84bfbd9232d3d69485b7f445c81e709ceacc3b3a/braidedSP/river.py#L17-L135)). This makes the stored outputs auditable but not independently rerunnable from the frozen repository alone.

#### WSE, branches, and width

The code derives `heightEGM = height - geoid - solid_earth_tide - load_tide_fes - pole_tide`, then takes unweighted `nanmedian` values inside a flat-ended 100 m-radius buffer around the already hull-clipped transect, giving nominally 200 m along-channel support. Selection uses strict `contains`, so points exactly on the buffer boundary are excluded ([reader](https://github.com/selfr-eo/braidedSP/blob/84bfbd9232d3d69485b7f445c81e709ceacc3b3a/braidedSP/swot.py#L103-L132), [node aggregation](https://github.com/selfr-eo/braidedSP/blob/84bfbd9232d3d69485b7f445c81e709ceacc3b3a/braidedSP/swot.py#L295-L339)). No quality, pixel-area, or water-fraction weighting and no explicit height outlier filter are applied.

braidedSP has two distinct mask paths. For centerline topology, `Mask.process` dilates/smooths the water mask, retains its largest connected component, fills configured small holes, skeletonizes, connects endpoints, prunes, and labels it ([`Mask.process`](https://github.com/selfr-eo/braidedSP/blob/84bfbd9232d3d69485b7f445c81e709ceacc3b3a/braidedSP/mask.py#L55-L100), [`_smooth`](https://github.com/selfr-eo/braidedSP/blob/84bfbd9232d3d69485b7f445c81e709ceacc3b3a/braidedSP/mask.py#L203-L254)). For PIXC selection, `process_extraction_mask` instead dilates the **original** mask by a five-pixel default and retains the largest component; it does not reuse the hole-filled centerline mask ([code](https://github.com/selfr-eo/braidedSP/blob/84bfbd9232d3d69485b7f445c81e709ceacc3b3a/braidedSP/mask.py#L174-L183)). Short centerlines are merged by proximity and cosine similarity ([`Centerline.merge_short_centerlines`](https://github.com/selfr-eo/braidedSP/blob/84bfbd9232d3d69485b7f445c81e709ceacc3b3a/braidedSP/centerline.py#L61-L156)). Branch IDs are observation-local; the study manually matches principal branches across dates using date-keyed branch dictionaries ([notebook](https://github.com/selfr-eo/braidedSP/blob/84bfbd9232d3d69485b7f445c81e709ceacc3b3a/publication/notebooks/view_BraidedSP.ipynb?plain=1#L1116-L1166)). The comma-separated downstream-ID field is not a graph or explicit island model ([`join_cl_at_joints`](https://github.com/selfr-eo/braidedSP/blob/84bfbd9232d3d69485b7f445c81e709ceacc3b3a/braidedSP/centerline.py#L160-L262)).

At 200 m node spacing, `np.arange(0, length, 200)` includes the branch start and excludes its terminal endpoint. Each line is perpendicular to the local sampled-centerline tangent. A branch corridor selects points; a concave hull with `allow_holes=True` uses ratio 0.01, or 0.05 when branch length exceeds 3 km; and width is `length(transect ∩ hull)`. Multipart pieces contribute their summed length. The next **per-side centerline-buffer distance**, not the transect length, is `round(1.1 × median node width)`, so the full corridor is approximately 2.2 times that median; convergence is a ≤50 m change or five iterations ([`_get_RiverSP`](https://github.com/selfr-eo/braidedSP/blob/84bfbd9232d3d69485b7f445c81e709ceacc3b3a/braidedSP/swot.py#L176-L374), [`create_points`](https://github.com/selfr-eo/braidedSP/blob/84bfbd9232d3d69485b7f445c81e709ceacc3b3a/braidedSP/tools.py#L571-L576)). Publication code uses flat channel-corridor caps, while current packaged `tools.trim_to_one_channel` uses Shapely's default round caps ([publication helper](https://github.com/selfr-eo/braidedSP/blob/84bfbd9232d3d69485b7f445c81e709ceacc3b3a/publication/tools/sp_utils.py#L44-L55), [package helper](https://github.com/selfr-eo/braidedSP/blob/84bfbd9232d3d69485b7f445c81e709ceacc3b3a/braidedSP/tools.py#L578-L593)). This is branch-conditioned only to the degree that the centerline buffer isolates a branch.

#### Paper/code differences and validation limits

- **Half-length ambiguity:** the paper calls 2 km low-flow and 3 km high-flow values a buffer/transect length; publication code applies them on **both** sides, producing 4 km and 6 km full lines ([helper](https://github.com/selfr-eo/braidedSP/blob/84bfbd9232d3d69485b7f445c81e709ceacc3b3a/publication/tools/sp_utils.py#L117-L148)).
- **Packaged default drift:** current `SWOT.cal_SP` always starts at 2,000 m, lacking the publication helper's high-flow 3,000 m branch ([package](https://github.com/selfr-eo/braidedSP/blob/84bfbd9232d3d69485b7f445c81e709ceacc3b3a/braidedSP/swot.py#L66-L69)).
- **Dark-water units:** paper text reports about −0.14 cm mean height bias while stored notebook output is −0.147 in meter-context output, with 2.31 m RMSE; the sources do not establish which unit is erroneous ([notebook](https://github.com/selfr-eo/braidedSP/blob/84bfbd9232d3d69485b7f445c81e709ceacc3b3a/publication/notebooks/sensitivity_analysis.ipynb?plain=1#L240-L262)).
- **Uncertainty naming:** the paper describes median `dH`, whereas the code stores separate medians `phstd_med` and `dhdp_med` and never calculates a field called `dH` ([aggregation code](https://github.com/selfr-eo/braidedSP/blob/84bfbd9232d3d69485b7f445c81e709ceacc3b3a/braidedSP/swot.py#L295-L339)). Its intended paper-to-code transformation cannot be inferred.
- **Profile breakpoint:** paper text describes about 24 km; the active analysis selection uses 23 km ([notebook](https://github.com/selfr-eo/braidedSP/blob/84bfbd9232d3d69485b7f445c81e709ceacc3b3a/publication/notebooks/view_BraidedSP.ipynb?plain=1#L1358-L1370)). This does not affect the width definition but is a reproducibility warning.
- **Neighbor capture:** the paper does not report high-flow width comparison because channels roughly 300 m apart can be drawn into the iterative corridor. Once included, the larger median makes the next corridor wider. This positive feedback is directly relevant to the Koshi ([paper](https://doi.org/10.1029/2025GL116488)).

For the low-flow comparison, `SWOT width - Sentinel-2 width` has mean 195.87 m, median 185.79 m, and standard deviation 254.59 m in the stored notebook ([analysis output](https://github.com/selfr-eo/braidedSP/blob/84bfbd9232d3d69485b7f445c81e709ceacc3b3a/publication/notebooks/width_analysis.ipynb?plain=1#L1329-L1362)). This is configuration-specific: the notebook uses 200 m node spacing, 1,000 m half-length transects, a fixed 1,000 m branch-buffer radius, hull ratio 0.01, and explicit intersection-length summation, rather than the current package's adaptive defaults ([configuration block](https://github.com/selfr-eo/braidedSP/blob/84bfbd9232d3d69485b7f445c81e709ceacc3b3a/publication/notebooks/width_analysis.ipynb?plain=1#L1125-L1243)). Nor is Sentinel-2 fully independent: its mask supplies centerline topology and PIXC extraction support as well as the comparison width. In the 1,079-node joined sensitivity comparison, adding class 3 to class 4 changes width by mean +26.2 m and RMSE 48.1 m and height by mean +0.0037 m and RMSE 0.0187 m; adding class 5 to classes 3+4 changes width by mean +14.5 m and RMSE 54.5 m and produces stored height mean −0.147 m and RMSE 2.31 m ([counts/merge](https://github.com/selfr-eo/braidedSP/blob/84bfbd9232d3d69485b7f445c81e709ceacc3b3a/publication/notebooks/sensitivity_analysis.ipynb?plain=1#L55-L88), [metrics](https://github.com/selfr-eo/braidedSP/blob/84bfbd9232d3d69485b7f445c81e709ceacc3b3a/publication/notebooks/sensitivity_analysis.ipynb?plain=1#L240-L262)).

For official RiverSP comparison only, the paper removes `node_q == 3` and NaNs but retains suspect (`1`) and degraded (`2`) nodes because good nodes are scarce; about 97% of retained low-flow nodes are suspect and all retained high-flow nodes are degraded ([paper](https://doi.org/10.1029/2025GL116488), [notebook filter](https://github.com/selfr-eo/braidedSP/blob/84bfbd9232d3d69485b7f445c81e709ceacc3b3a/publication/notebooks/view_BraidedSP.ipynb?plain=1#L1332-L1337)). This does not constitute braidedSP PIXC QC: braidedSP reads no PIXC quality words.

**Case-study findings, not validation metrics.** The paper reports low-flow branch slopes of 3.7/4.2 cm km⁻¹ before the profile break and 7.2/8.9 cm km⁻¹ after it, high-flow slopes of 5.8/5.5 cm km⁻¹, and a low-flow interbranch WSE difference approaching 0.5 m that becomes negligible at high flow ([paper](https://doi.org/10.1029/2025GL116488)). The study reports no gauge, field, or ICESat-2 WSE validation; this audit found no formal tests/CI in the frozen [repository tree](https://github.com/selfr-eo/braidedSP/tree/84bfbd9232d3d69485b7f445c81e709ceacc3b3a).

### 3.2 NASA/JPL RiverObs

#### Inputs and water definition

RiverObs is a production-oriented reach/node processor, not a free-form braided-river transect package. It requires a PIXC plus a prior reach database containing fixed node locations, reach boundaries, high-resolution centerlines, widths, and connectivity ([README](https://github.com/SWOTAlgorithms/RiverObs/blob/dc0c7fde6ccad17238090a003bea137ffb3d0663/README.md#L13-L31)). `L2PixcToRiverTile.do_river_processing` constructs `SWOTRiverEstimator`, loads the prior database, and calls `SWOTRiverEstimator.process_reaches`; improved geolocation and final product construction are separate subsequent methods ([river processing](https://github.com/SWOTAlgorithms/RiverObs/blob/dc0c7fde6ccad17238090a003bea137ffb3d0663/src/SWOTRiver/Estimate.py#L247-L366), [improved geolocation](https://github.com/SWOTAlgorithms/RiverObs/blob/dc0c7fde6ccad17238090a003bea137ffb3d0663/src/SWOTRiver/Estimate.py#L368-L400), [products](https://github.com/SWOTAlgorithms/RiverObs/blob/dc0c7fde6ccad17238090a003bea137ffb3d0663/src/SWOTRiver/Estimate.py#L427-L505)). It has no required optical, DEM, ICESat-2, or separate calibration dataset in the normal PIXC path. The normal CLI processes one PIXC tile; separate RiverSP assembly concatenates/sorts RiverTile arrays rather than making a raw multi-tile PIXC mosaic ([assembler](https://github.com/SWOTAlgorithms/RiverObs/blob/dc0c7fde6ccad17238090a003bea137ffb3d0663/src/bin/swot_rivertiles2riversp.py#L16-L69), [`RiverSP.from_rivertiles`](https://github.com/SWOTAlgorithms/RiverObs/blob/dc0c7fde6ccad17238090a003bea137ffb3d0663/src/SWOTRiver/products/riversp.py#L33-L124)).

The recommended command template loads PIXC classes 2–7. It uses `water_frac` for classes 2 and 3; the composite area algorithm treats open and low-coherence water as full-pixel interior water, class 3/2 as fractional edge candidates, and dark water as interior water ([template](https://github.com/SWOTAlgorithms/RiverObs/blob/dc0c7fde6ccad17238090a003bea137ffb3d0663/src/bin/swot_pixc2rivertile.py#L14-L56), [`AGG_CLASSES`](https://github.com/SWOTAlgorithms/RiverObs/blob/dc0c7fde6ccad17238090a003bea137ffb3d0663/src/SWOTWater/constants.py#L5-L44), [`area_only`](https://github.com/SWOTAlgorithms/RiverObs/blob/dc0c7fde6ccad17238090a003bea137ffb3d0663/src/SWOTWater/aggregate.py#L290-L367)). It computes a second detected area excluding dark classes as well as the dark-inclusive area ([`RiverNode.area_with_uncert`](https://github.com/SWOTAlgorithms/RiverObs/blob/dc0c7fde6ccad17238090a003bea137ffb3d0663/src/RiverObs/RiverNode.py#L384-L429)).

The template supplies bit masks for classification, geolocation, and `sig0` quality words and excludes bright land. The estimator uses bitwise intersections, distinguishes suspect/degraded/bad uses, and can fall back to broader pixels when a node has too few good/suspect measurements ([quality setup/application](https://github.com/SWOTAlgorithms/RiverObs/blob/dc0c7fde6ccad17238090a003bea137ffb3d0663/src/SWOTRiver/SWOTRiverEstimator.py#L400-L646), [aggregation fallback](https://github.com/SWOTAlgorithms/RiverObs/blob/dc0c7fde6ccad17238090a003bea137ffb3d0663/src/SWOTRiver/SWOTRiverEstimator.py#L1367-L1378)). The reviewed template sets thresholds of 20 good/suspect WSE pixels, 9,999 good/suspect area pixels, and 80% good/suspect reach coverage; area nodes will therefore ordinarily use the broader surviving pool rather than reach 9,999. If any initial read of `classification_qual`, `geolocation_qual`, or `bright_land_flag` raises `KeyError`, code warns and substitutes zero arrays for all three for assignment ([missing-variable path](https://github.com/SWOTAlgorithms/RiverObs/blob/dc0c7fde6ccad17238090a003bea137ffb3d0663/src/SWOTRiver/SWOTRiverEstimator.py#L410-L447)). These configured aggregate masks and fail-open missing-variable behavior are not the same abstraction as SWOT PIXC Lab's complete metadata-driven bit decoder and per-reason pixel audit.

The reviewed CLI template's exact aggregate masks are:

- `geo_qual_wse_suspect = 0x0000effc`, `geo_qual_wse_degraded = 0x01fb0000`, and `geo_qual_wse_bad = 0xfe040000`;
- `class_qual_area_suspect = 0x0000fff0`, `class_qual_area_degraded = 0x01fb0000`, and `class_qual_area_bad = 0xfe040008`;
- `sig0_qual_suspect = 0x01ffffff` and `sig0_qual_bad = 0xfe000000`.

RiverObs intersects those hexadecimal masks with the PIXC words; it does not decode and retain every CF `flag_meaning` as a named, per-pixel reason ([template](https://github.com/SWOTAlgorithms/RiverObs/blob/dc0c7fde6ccad17238090a003bea137ffb3d0663/src/bin/swot_pixc2rivertile.py#L49-L56), [application](https://github.com/SWOTAlgorithms/RiverObs/blob/dc0c7fde6ccad17238090a003bea137ffb3d0663/src/SWOTRiver/SWOTRiverEstimator.py#L422-L551)). These are frozen example-template values, not recommendations or proof of the operational Version D parameter set.

A reproducibility caveat is important: `SWOTRiverEstimator.__init__` defaults, the nearby docstring, and the CLI template do not all contain the same `use_heights` vector ([constructor](https://github.com/SWOTAlgorithms/RiverObs/blob/dc0c7fde6ccad17238090a003bea137ffb3d0663/src/SWOTRiver/SWOTRiverEstimator.py#L95-L138), [actual arguments](https://github.com/SWOTAlgorithms/RiverObs/blob/dc0c7fde6ccad17238090a003bea137ffb3d0663/src/SWOTRiver/SWOTRiverEstimator.py#L250-L333), [CLI template](https://github.com/SWOTAlgorithms/RiverObs/blob/dc0c7fde6ccad17238090a003bea137ffb3d0663/src/bin/swot_pixc2rivertile.py#L14-L56)). A Phase 5 comparison must record the exact operational parameter file rather than saying only “RiverObs defaults.”

**Official Version D context, separate from public-code evidence.** The mission release note describes PGD0 reprocessing through 2025-04-27 and PID0 forward production beginning 2025-04-28, with FES2022b tides and PRD/SWORD v17b. It also says HR uncertainty estimates remain unvalidated and notes possible extra-pixel river assignment/spurious heights and rare duplicate RiverSP records ([Version D release note](https://archive.podaac.earthdata.nasa.gov/podaac-ops-cumulus-docs/web-misc/swot_mission_docs/SWOT_VersionD_KaRIn_Products_Release_Note_20250423b.pdf)). These facts characterize released products; they do not prove that the frozen public `develop` commit and its example template exactly reproduce the operational SDS configuration.

#### WSE, reach assignment, and width

For assigned pixels with `-200 < geoid < 200`, the estimator subtracts `geoid + solid_earth_tide + load_tide_fes + pole_tide` from the input `height`; missing correction arrays trigger a warning, so a complete correction is not unconditional ([code](https://github.com/SWOTAlgorithms/RiverObs/blob/dc0c7fde6ccad17238090a003bea137ffb3d0663/src/SWOTRiver/SWOTRiverEstimator.py#L1325-L1345)). The official PIXC `height` is WGS84-ellipsoid height with applicable instrument, atmospheric/ionospheric, and crossover corrections already applied; those must not be applied again ([PIXC PDD](https://archive.podaac.earthdata.nasa.gov/podaac-ops-cumulus-docs/web-misc/swot_mission_docs/pdd/D-56411_SWOT_Product_Description_L2_HR_PIXC_20250224a_RevC_clean_sig_final.pdf)). Default `height_agg_method='weight'` applies inverse pixel-height-variance weighting, where pixel height standard deviation is `abs(phase_noise_std × dheight_dphase)`; median and uniform alternatives exist and uncertainty terms are propagated ([`RiverNode`](https://github.com/SWOTAlgorithms/RiverObs/blob/dc0c7fde6ccad17238090a003bea137ffb3d0663/src/RiverObs/RiverNode.py#L347-L373), [`aggregate.height_only`](https://github.com/SWOTAlgorithms/RiverObs/blob/dc0c7fde6ccad17238090a003bea137ffb3d0663/src/SWOTWater/aggregate.py#L99-L145)). Configurable piecewise/iterative outlier processing and Bayesian reach reconstruction operate beyond simple node aggregation; their use depends on the external configuration ([outlier code](https://github.com/SWOTAlgorithms/RiverObs/blob/dc0c7fde6ccad17238090a003bea137ffb3d0663/src/SWOTRiver/SWOTRiverEstimator.py#L2672-L2915), [reconstruction](https://github.com/SWOTAlgorithms/RiverObs/blob/dc0c7fde6ccad17238090a003bea137ffb3d0663/src/SWOTRiver/SWOTRiverEstimator.py#L3159-L3336)).

PIXC pixels are projected into along-reach and normal coordinates relative to prior centerlines. Segmentation uses default two-dimensional four-neighbor labeling in azimuth/range space; a 3×3 grey dilation attaches zero-label/land-edge cells, with an arbitrary larger-integer-label outcome when two components touch according to the source comment ([`segment_water_class`](https://github.com/SWOTAlgorithms/RiverObs/blob/dc0c7fde6ccad17238090a003bea137ffb3d0663/src/SWOTRiver/SWOTRiverEstimator.py#L706-L753)). All component labels already inside the initial node window can remain, but only its dominant label may extend into the larger “extreme” window. Competing reach assignments are resolved by closest prior node/centerline distance ([`flag_out_channel_and_label`](https://github.com/SWOTAlgorithms/RiverObs/blob/dc0c7fde6ccad17238090a003bea137ffb3d0663/src/RiverObs/RiverObs.py#L148-L269), [`assign_reaches`](https://github.com/SWOTAlgorithms/RiverObs/blob/dc0c7fde6ccad17238090a003bea137ffb3d0663/src/SWOTRiver/SWOTRiverEstimator.py#L946-L1163)). The code notes that prior width underestimates outer extent where bars/islands occur and expands the later search after choosing a dominant label ([comments and implementation](https://github.com/SWOTAlgorithms/RiverObs/blob/dc0c7fde6ccad17238090a003bea137ffb3d0663/src/SWOTRiver/SWOTRiverEstimator.py#L1030-L1051)).

For node $i$, RiverObs reports

$$
w_{\mathrm{area},i}=\frac{\sum_{p\in i}A_{\mathrm{wet},p}}{\Delta s_i},
$$

where $\Delta s_i$ is the node's along-reach support length and $A_{\mathrm{wet},p}$ is produced by the configured simple, water-fraction, or composite estimator. The executable definition is [`RiverNode.width_area`](https://github.com/SWOTAlgorithms/RiverObs/blob/dc0c7fde6ccad17238090a003bea137ffb3d0663/src/RiverObs/RiverNode.py#L324-L338) and the uncertainty-aware path is [`area_with_uncert`](https://github.com/SWOTAlgorithms/RiverObs/blob/dc0c7fde6ccad17238090a003bea137ffb3d0663/src/RiverObs/RiverNode.py#L384-L429). `width_ptp = max(n)-min(n)` exists as a generic statistic, but the nominal output field `w_area` is populated with area-derived width ([node fields](https://github.com/SWOTAlgorithms/RiverObs/blob/dc0c7fde6ccad17238090a003bea137ffb3d0663/src/SWOTRiver/SWOTRiverEstimator.py#L1928-L1967)).

At reach scale, RiverObs separately computes

$$
W_{\mathrm{reach}}=
\frac{\sum_{i\in M_A}A_i}{\sum_{i\in M_A}L_i},
\qquad
A_{\mathrm{reported}}=W_{\mathrm{reach}}\sum_iL_i,
$$

so partially observed valid-node area can be converted to width over observed valid length and then extrapolated across the full prior-reach length. The detected-area output follows a distinct sum rather than that same extrapolation ([`process_reach`](https://github.com/SWOTAlgorithms/RiverObs/blob/dc0c7fde6ccad17238090a003bea137ffb3d0663/src/SWOTRiver/SWOTRiverEstimator.py#L2017-L2085)). This reach aggregate is also neither a one-line wet-length sum nor outer-bank span.

**Audit inference.** In a water–island–water node support region, wet areas on both sides can contribute to the numerator while the dry island does not. This behaves like an area-averaged sum of wetted arms if they are assigned to the same reach/node. It is not exactly a one-dimensional sum at one transect. A disconnected arm inside the initial window can remain even with a separate label; an arm outside that window is omitted unless it shares the dominant label and lies within the extreme-distance window. A separately represented SWORD reach may instead receive it independently. Thus RiverObs output cannot be mapped to “total multi-channel width” without inspecting reach topology and pixel assignment.

### 3.3 swot_for_flood

#### Inputs and flood classification

**Paper.** The flood study uses PIXC Version C; Sentinel-1/2 reference maps generated with FloodML; ESA WorldCover 2021 strata; 5 m RGE ALTI at Chinon and 30 m FABDEM elsewhere; HAND/manual floodplain delineation; and four non-flood PIXC observations per site to build dry means ([paper](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.rse.2025.115101), [archived data](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.15848842)). It retains PIXC classes 3, 4, and 5 for its water mask and states that low-coherence/specular-ringing pixels are discarded. It does not use SWORD, RiverSP, or ICESat-2 for the flood method.

**Current code.** `search_PIXC` now requests `SWOT_L2_HR_PIXC_D`, so the downloader has moved to Version D while the study methods/results remain Version C ([`core/downloader.py::search_PIXC`](https://github.com/quentinbns/swot_for_flood/blob/0003b28568d29a3e5d077c033425d3e520e9fa41/core/downloader.py#L74-L121)). This is product-version drift, not evidence that the published thresholds were validated on Version D.

`Rasterizer.pixc_to_gpkg` opens root, `/pixel_cloud`, and `/noise`, then reduces selected values to a GeoPackage ([code](https://github.com/quentinbns/swot_for_flood/blob/0003b28568d29a3e5d077c033425d3e520e9fa41/core/pixc_rasterizer.py#L153-L272)). Its actual rejection expression is `(classification in {6, 7}) AND (interferogram_qual == 524288) AND (bright_land_flag == 1)`, followed by inversion to a keep mask. It tests exact equality rather than decoding a bitfield. If the optional `darkwater` path is enabled, the code ORs that keep mask with `classification != 5`. This cannot remove class 5 because class-5 pixels could not satisfy the class-6/7 rejection and are already kept; it also restores any rejected non-class-5 pixel. Thus the advertised dark-water filter is ineffective in this frozen operation sequence. Those operations do not implement a plain translation of the paper's stated class 3/4/5 selection. The same function clips with `within` and drops duplicate coordinate pairs, losing boundary points and silently keeping the first duplicate—both behaviors conflict with SWOT PIXC Lab's Phase 2 policy.

`gpkg_to_tiff` uses GDAL inverse-distance gridding (defaults: power 2, smoothing 1, radius 50, maximum 20 points, nodata −9999). The condition `if var != "classification" or var != "bright_land_flag"` is always true, so the intended categorical nearest-neighbor branch is unreachable ([code](https://github.com/quentinbns/swot_for_flood/blob/0003b28568d29a3e5d077c033425d3e520e9fa41/core/pixc_rasterizer.py#L274-L338)). This is a code finding, not a criticism of the published conceptual flood method; it means the frozen implementation should not be reused verbatim.

#### Extent method and validation

`SwotMean.compute_mean` creates an arithmetic dry-date mean. `SwotCollection.create_flood_mask` applies user-supplied, land-cover-specific thresholds to total coherence, coherent power, `sig0`, or dry-date differences, labels low-coherence/low-SNR uncertainty, and applies morphological filters ([dry mean](https://github.com/quentinbns/swot_for_flood/blob/0003b28568d29a3e5d077c033425d3e520e9fa41/core/swot_raster.py#L303-L315), [mask creation](https://github.com/quentinbns/swot_for_flood/blob/0003b28568d29a3e5d077c033425d3e520e9fa41/core/swot_raster.py#L1090-L1261)). This is a raster flood-extent classifier; no centerline, transect, branch, island, width, corrected WSE, or branch-level WSE is produced.

The paper evaluates Chinon, Porto Alegre, Owensboro, and Farkadona against near-coincident Sentinel-derived/manual references. In that site order, Table 8 reports CSI percentages against manually delineated reference of: total coherence 64.92/42.54/48.42/20.36; coherent power 69.98/29.38/47.57/24.51; `sig0` 62.64/28.22/48.95/26.61; PIXC classification 59.55/22.95/40.14/24.44; and FloodML 30.91/70.55/34.90/42.40 ([paper](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.rse.2025.115101)). The reference is not an independent surveyed bank line, and the thresholds vary by site and land-cover class. The method is informative for future extent uncertainty but supplies no validated channel-width estimator.

### 3.4 rulesofriveravulsion / Gearon–Edmonds workflow

This project is methodologically adjacent, not a SWOT package. The paper studies 58 rivers using ICESat-2 and FABDEM topography, RiverATLAS discharge, and BASED-estimated bankfull depth ([paper](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-07964-2)). ATL03 photons provide dense along-track elevations and ATL08 supplies 100 m segment classifications mapped to those photons. Cross sections near avulsion nodes and ridge/floodplain/WSE levels are manually interpreted; main-channel slope and superelevation relationships are the target—not wetted branch width.

The paper selects cross sections within roughly ten channel widths of an avulsion site, favors tracks within about 20° of perpendicular, and groups repeat estimates within roughly three widths. It takes repeated slope/superelevation measurements and propagates ICESat-2 WSE uncertainty with a triangular ±0.25 m distribution in 10,000 Monte Carlo realizations ([paper](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-07964-2)). Those are useful validation-design ideas, not reusable automatic transect-construction code.

`cross_section_sideview.py` groups photons by `rgt`, `cycle`, `track`, and `pair`; constructs plotted distance as `segment_dist + x_atc` (or uses an existing `distance`); optionally applies caller-supplied `atl08_class` and `atl03_cnf`; and overlays stored manually interpreted avulsion-ridge/floodplain/WSE values ([source](https://github.com/jameshgrn/rulesofriveravulsion/blob/e63e5dfcdca7ecb2d06554f718a04c0cac0cb5e2/figure_plotting/cross_section_sideview.py#L23-L95)). It does **not** generate a perpendicular transect, detect banks, split branches, or calculate width. README explains ATL08 class and ATL03 confidence choices but offers them as interactive analyst choices ([README](https://github.com/jameshgrn/rulesofriveravulsion/blob/e63e5dfcdca7ecb2d06554f718a04c0cac0cb5e2/README.md#L167-L194)).

The data dictionary only labels `width_m` as channel width and gives no derivation ([source](https://github.com/jameshgrn/rulesofriveravulsion/blob/e63e5dfcdca7ecb2d06554f718a04c0cac0cb5e2/data/manuscript_data/datadictionary_supplement.txt#L1-L32)). Accordingly, this review does not attribute a width algorithm to it. README also references `cross_section_mapview.py`, but that file is absent at the frozen commit—a documentation/code reproducibility gap.

Published method checks in Extended Data Figures 2–3 include FABDEM-versus-ICESat-2 height-above-river comparisons (MAE 0.59 m, RMSE 0.71 m, $R^2 \approx 0.92$), slope comparison (MAE 0.0056 m/m, RMSE 0.007 m/m, $R^2 = 0.83$), and a 762-case BASED depth comparison (MAE 0.33 m, $R^2 = 0.89$, MAPE 20%). The depth RMSE is about 1.04 m in the paper text and about 1.02 m in its figure caption, so this review does not silently choose between them ([paper](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-07964-2)). These validate elevation/slope/depth parts of that avulsion study, not PIXC water classification, banks, or channel widths.

- “Used for area,” “used for WSE,” and “used to connect a component” are separate roles in RiverObs. A single boolean water mask would lose that distinction.
- The RiverObs CLI template and estimator constructor select different WSE classes. Any benchmark must archive the actual parameter file and product CRID.
- In swot_for_flood, the `interferogram_qual == 524288` equality is only one term in the three-way rejection conjunction. The optional dark-water OR makes every pixel pass that local keep mask rather than removing class 5. The paper's prose instead describes excluding low-coherence/specular-ringing observations. This is an explicit paper/code difference, so neither behavior should be inferred from the other ([code](https://github.com/quentinbns/swot_for_flood/blob/0003b28568d29a3e5d077c033425d3e520e9fa41/core/pixc_rasterizer.py#L184-L207)).

This is a stronger basis for Version D auditability than copying aggregate hexadecimal constants from one RiverObs revision.

### 5.2 Design implication

Phase 5 needs at least two explicit masks:

1. **extent eligibility**, which answers whether a pixel may support a wet boundary or wet area;
2. **height eligibility**, which answers whether its height may enter branch WSE statistics.

RiverObs demonstrates why these differ; braidedSP's dark-water experiment demonstrates the cost of conflating them. Any later bank/width result should carry the input profile name, profile parameters, all per-reason exclusions, and class counts before/after. This is a design conclusion from the comparison, not a new QC rule.

## 8. Width-definition comparison

### 8.1 The three required estimands

For one ordered transect coordinate $s$, suppose the detected wet set is a union of disjoint intervals

$$
\mathcal{W}=\bigcup_{j=1}^{m}[a_j,b_j].
$$

Then three distinct quantities are:

1. **Individual branch width:** $w_j=b_j-a_j$, but only after interval $j$ has a defensible branch identity.
2. **Total wetted width:** $W_{\mathrm{wet}}=\sum_j(b_j-a_j)$.
3. **Outer-bank/channel-belt span:** $W_{\mathrm{span}}=\max_j b_j-\min_j a_j$, which includes islands, exposed bars, and all dry gaps between the outermost wet intervals.

The term “valley width” should be avoided for $W_{\mathrm{span}}$ unless actual valley margins are mapped; a wide alluvial river belt is not necessarily the geomorphic valley.

### 8.2 Simple numerical example

| Ordered interval | Station range (m) | State | Contribution to individual width | Contribution to total wetted width | Contribution to outer span |
|---|---:|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Branch A | 0–120 | Wet | 120 m | 120 m | Sets left edge at 0 m |
| Island | 120–180 | Dry | — | 0 m | Included as 60 m gap |
| Branch B | 180–260 | Wet | 80 m | 80 m | Included |
| Bar | 260–310 | Dry | — | 0 m | Included as 50 m gap |
| Branch C | 310–350 | Wet | 40 m | 40 m | Sets right edge at 350 m |
| **Result** | | | **A=120, B=80, C=40 m** | **240 m** | **350 m** |

There is an additional fourth quantity: **area-equivalent width** $W_A=A_{\mathrm{wet}}/L$ over a two-dimensional support length $L$. It approaches total wetted width for straight, parallel, stationary arms sampled uniformly over $L$, but curvature, confluences, oblique branches, variable width, assignment boundaries, and missing components make it different from a one-line $W_{\mathrm{wet}}$.

### 8.3 Which method calculates what

| Method/output | Individual branch width | Total wetted width | Outer span | Area-equivalent width |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| braidedSP `transect.intersection(concave_hull).length` | **Conditionally:** one scalar per generated branch centerline, but multipart intersection pieces are not returned separately and neighboring arms can enter the branch hull | **Summed hull-intersection proxy within the selected corridor:** Shapely length sums multipart intersections, but the hull may bridge dry gaps or omit sparse water | No | No |
| RiverObs nominal `w_area` | Only if one prior reach uniquely represents one physical branch and assignment is isolated; not guaranteed | Approximately over a node support when all arms map to one reach; not a literal transect value | No | **Yes**, by definition |
| swot_for_flood | No | No | No | No |
| rulesofriveravulsion | No implemented estimator | No | No | No |
| SWOT PIXC Lab Phase 4 | No; returns points and stations only | No | No | No |

Neither the generic RiverObs `width_ptp` function nor raw `max(station)-min(station)` should be adopted as a validated bank width. With irregular PIXC sampling, outliers or isolated classifications can expand the span, while missing bank pixels can contract it. `width_ptp` exists in [`RiverNode`](https://github.com/SWOTAlgorithms/RiverObs/blob/dc0c7fde6ccad17238090a003bea137ffb3d0663/src/RiverObs/RiverNode.py#L302-L322), but RiverObs's nominal product path uses area-derived width.

### 8.4 Koshi conceptual interpretation

Consider one Phase 4 Koshi transect encountering branch A → island → branch B → bar → branch C. This table describes expected semantics, not a result from running a width implementation.

| Method | Branch A | Island | Branch B | Bar | Branch C | Output width concept |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| braidedSP, centerline assigned to A only | Intended to form A's hull/intersection | Centerline-mask hole filling can change topology/orientation; the dry island is excluded from width only if the PIXC concave hull preserves its hole/gap | Should be outside A's corridor, but can be captured if the adaptive buffer expands | Same hull ambiguity | Same neighbor-capture risk | Sum of hull-intersection pieces inside the A-conditioned corridor; labeled as A but not guaranteed to contain only A or only observed wet surface |
| braidedSP, one corridor encompasses all arms | Included in the hull calculation | Excluded only if the PIXC concave hull preserves the dry hole/gap; centerline and extraction masks follow separate paths | Included | Same condition | Included | One summed hull-intersection proxy that may approach $W_{\mathrm{wet}}$ only when every wet arm and dry gap is represented; component widths and gaps are not retained |
| RiverObs, all arms retained in the same reach/node support | Its assigned wet area contributes | No wet area; edge fractions may contribute | Its assigned wet area contributes even if it has a separate component label inside the initial window | No wet area | Its assigned wet area contributes | $A_{\mathrm{wet}}/\Delta s$, approximately a support-averaged sum of arms; not $W_{\mathrm{span}}$ |
| RiverObs, branches represented/assigned separately | Contributes to A's prior reach/node | No identity | Contributes to B's reach/node | No identity | Contributes to C's reach/node | Separate area-equivalent reach/node widths, subject to prior topology and assignment; no cross-reach summed transect product |
| swot_for_flood | May be flood/water raster | Dry or later morphologically bridged depending settings | May be flood/water raster | Dry or bridged | May be flood/water raster | Flood extent only; no branch width |
| rulesofriveravulsion | Photons could be visually interpreted | Visible topographic high if sampled | Photons could be interpreted | Visible profile feature | Photons could be interpreted | Manual elevation profile; audited code computes no wet interval or width |
| Proposed Phase 5 estimand A | 120 m in the example | Explicit 60 m dry gap | 80 m | Explicit 50 m dry gap | 40 m | Three separate widths with branch identifiers |
| Proposed Phase 5 estimand B | 120 m | Excluded | 80 m | Excluded | 40 m | $W_{\mathrm{wet}}=240$ m |
| Proposed Phase 5 estimand C | Left outer edge | Included | Included | Included | Right outer edge | $W_{\mathrm{span}}=350$ m |

The essential data-model requirement is therefore to return the ordered interval table first. The three scalars can be derived from it; the reverse is impossible.

### Minimum future Koshi validation matrix

No option should be called research-grade until it is tested across:

- at least several Koshi observations spanning low and high water, both 107L and 108L where relevant;
- manual interval/bank interpretation on co-registered, near-date high-resolution or Sentinel-2 imagery with uncertainty in temporal mismatch;
- independent transects in single-channel, island-separated, bar-separated, confluence, bifurcation, and tile-edge settings;
- all candidate extent class profiles, including explicit dark-water and low-coherence sensitivity;
- corridor width, transect angle, point-gap tolerance, bank rule, raster/hull resolution if any, and optical-mask age;
- repeat annotations by more than one analyst for a subset, so reference uncertainty is measured;
- provenance checks showing that tile combination neither duplicates nor silently removes pixels;
- comparisons of individual intervals, total wetted width, outer span, and area-equivalent width without treating one as truth for another.

Where possible, field GNSS/UAS or suitable orthophoto bank observations should supplement satellite imagery. ICESat-2 can validate elevation/cross-section context where tracks coincide, but it cannot by itself validate a two-dimensional same-date wetted boundary.

### 12.2 Ideas to evaluate, not yet adopt as defaults

- **From RiverObs:** class-specific extent/WSE roles; composite area and `water_frac`; area-equivalent width with uncertainty; connected-component diagnostics; prior topology; and an operational comparator output. These should be implemented independently and transparently if selected.
- **From braidedSP:** dynamic centerlines for multiple channels; explicit perpendicular nodes; branch-conditioned analysis; optical-mask sensitivity; and adaptive-corridor diagnostics. Phase 5 should additionally preserve and order each transect–hull intersection geometry, unlike braidedSP's packaged scalar-only output.
- **From swot_for_flood:** independent dry-state comparisons, land-cover-stratified validation, uncertainty maps, and CSI/F1/Kappa for extent—after correcting paper/code ambiguity and without importing its destructive dedup/raster path.
- **From rulesofriveravulsion:** analyst-reviewable cross-section figures, near-perpendicular track selection, repeated manual measurements, and Monte Carlo propagation of interpretation uncertainty.

### 12.3 Behaviors not to adopt

- Never silently retain only the largest connected component, fill islands, join endpoints, prune branches, or deduplicate coordinates.
- Never call point-cloud min/max spread a validated bank width.
- Never use a spatially broad optical mask as an unrecorded override of PIXC classes.
- Never hard-code Version C class/QC meanings into Version D processing without reading official metadata.
- Never mix extent eligibility, WSE eligibility, and segmentation eligibility into one undocumented boolean.
- Never allow an adaptive buffer to converge after capturing another branch without a diagnostic/failure state.
- Never discard ordered component widths and gaps after producing only a summed scalar.
- Never use interpolated categorical rasters through a continuous inverse-distance branch.

## 13. Three possible Phase 5 algorithm architectures

These are alternatives for discussion. No option is selected, ranked, or implemented.

### Option A — conservative/simple baseline

**Concept.** Extend the existing user-defined transect result with a transparent, analyst-reviewed wet-interval estimator. Require the user to supply the transect and explicitly choose a Version D extent-QC profile. Produce ordered candidate wet intervals plus all three estimands; do not assign persistent branch identities automatically.

| Dimension | Proposal |
|---|---|
| Scientific assumptions | Within a narrow corridor, accepted PIXC samples provide enough support to infer intersections with the wetted surface; analyst placement supplies meaningful orientation; bank uncertainty can be reported from gaps/resolution rather than hidden. |
| Required inputs | Existing `PixcObservation` or `PixcQCResult`; user LineString; corridor width; explicit extent profile; declared point-gap/boundary rule; optional image only for validation, not required computation. |
| Output model | Ordered interval records with start/end station, width, point count, class/QC counts, gap/bank uncertainty, and source provenance; separately named `individual_candidate_widths`, `total_wetted_width`, and `outer_wetted_span`. “Branch” remains candidate/analyst label. |
| Strengths | Minimal new topology assumptions; directly builds on Phase 4; easiest to test synthetically; every scalar can be reconstructed from intervals; failures are visible. |
| Failure modes | Sparse/irregular PIXC cannot uniquely identify banks; corridor may combine nearby longitudinal positions; oblique user transect biases width; isolated false water can inflate outer span; no automatic consistency along river/time. |
| Validation needs | Synthetic intervals/gaps/outliers; manual Koshi transects spanning simple and multi-arm cases; angle/corridor/class/gap sensitivity; imagery and repeat-analyst uncertainty. |
| Implementation complexity | Low to moderate. Main complexity is a scientifically defensible interval/bank rule and uncertainty model, not software plumbing. |

### Option B — braided-river-aware method

**Concept.** Add an explicit external/current water-extent layer and derive observation-specific channel centerlines/branches, influenced by braidedSP but preserving every intermediate geometry. Generate perpendicular transects per candidate branch, stop corridors at competing branches/components, and retain multipart wet intervals before any scalar aggregation.

| Dimension | Proposal |
|---|---|
| Scientific assumptions | A co-registered optical or validated SWOT-derived water mask represents branch topology close enough in time; skeleton branches correspond to hydraulically meaningful channels after documented scale choices; local perpendicular sections are meaningful away from junctions. |
| Required inputs | All Option A inputs plus a dated water mask (e.g., Sentinel-2) with cloud/temporal metadata; optional SWORD for orientation/reference; user seed/main flow direction; morphology and minimum-feature parameters. |
| Output model | Versioned geometry lineage: source mask → components/holes → skeleton → graph-like branch candidates → node/transect → ordered wet intervals. Preserve islands/holes and removed/merged candidates in an audit table. Branch IDs scoped by observation unless separately matched. |
| Strengths | Targets individual anabranches; can quantify branches, summed wet width, and belt span; supports branch-specific WSE summaries; optical geometry helps where PIXC is sparse. |
| Failure modes | Cloud/time mismatch, seasonal bars, optical threshold bias, morphology erasing islands, skeleton instability, junction orientation, cross-date ID instability, neighbor capture, and circularity if the same mask both defines and validates width. |
| Validation needs | Independent imagery dates/sensors or held-out masks; parameter sweeps for NDWI/morphology/pruning/hulls; branch-by-branch manual annotations; confluence/bifurcation exclusion rules; high-flow neighbor-capture stress tests; Version D QC/WSE assessment. |
| Implementation complexity | High. Requires raster/vector topology, branch-scoped provenance, geometry lineage, spatial indexing, and review tools. |

### Option C — advanced future graph/segmentation method

**Concept.** Build a probabilistic, time-aware wetted-network graph directly from PIXC plus optional imagery/priors. Model pixels or spatial cells as evidence with class/QC/area uncertainty; infer banks, holes, branches, splits/rejoins, and temporal branch lineage. Derive widths as posterior distributions for explicitly named estimands.

| Dimension | Proposal |
|---|---|
| Scientific assumptions | A probabilistic observation model can represent PIXC classification/geolocation gaps and ancillary disagreement; topology can be inferred without forcing a single deterministic mask; temporal regularization is valid at the chosen cadence. |
| Required inputs | Version D PIXC and complete metadata/QC; optional optical imagery, SWORD/PRD, DEM, and previous observations; calibrated likelihoods for class/QC/geolocation/edge evidence. |
| Output model | Spatial-temporal graph with branch/edge/node lineage, explicit island/hole objects, posterior bank positions, split/rejoin events, and distributions for branch width, total wet width, outer span, area-equivalent width, and branch WSE. |
| Strengths | Represents ambiguity instead of hiding it; potentially robust to gaps and changing branch topology; enables temporal anabranch science and principled uncertainty. |
| Failure modes | Model identifiability, dependence on unvalidated priors, computational cost, training/calibration data scarcity, false confidence, difficult reproducibility, and scope expansion far beyond an initial Phase 5. |
| Validation needs | Large multi-river annotated benchmark; withheld sites/dates; calibration/reliability tests; topology-event scoring; field/imagery/ICESat-2 data where applicable; ablations for every ancillary source; uncertainty coverage tests. |
| Implementation complexity | Very high/research program. It should be staged only after an independently validated Option A-like measurement contract exists. |

Cross-cutting requirements for any option:

- raw points and Phase 2 provenance remain immutable;
- extent, WSE, topology, and visualization are separate layers;
- result names state the estimand and vertical datum;
- ordered wet/dry intervals precede scalar summaries;
- all parameters, source dates/CRIDs, QC profiles, transformations, and failure states are serializable;
- algorithms fail explicitly when data cannot distinguish banks/branches;
- real-data validation is optional in CI, while synthetic geometry and regression fixtures are mandatory.

## 14. Scientific decisions that require the project owner

### Estimand and reporting

1. Is the primary Phase 5 endpoint individual branch width, total wetted width, outer wetted span, area-equivalent width, or a required bundle of all four?
2. Does “branch” require persistent identity along a reach and across dates, or is an ordered wet segment at one user transect sufficient initially?
3. Should bars/islands below a stated scale be reported as gaps, merged into a branch, or offered as parameterized alternatives?
4. At confluences/bifurcations, should width be withheld, assigned to several branches, or reported only as total wet width/span?

### Evidence and ancillary inputs

5. May Phase 5 require near-date Sentinel-2/other imagery, or must the baseline run from PIXC alone?
6. If imagery is allowed, what temporal offset, cloud threshold, water index/classifier, resolution, and georegistration tolerance are acceptable?
7. Is SWORD/PRD a reference/orientation aid, a mandatory branch prior, or only an external comparator for the Koshi?
8. Which independent data can the owner supply for Koshi validation: manual banks, UAS/orthophoto, field GNSS, gauges, ICESat-2 tracks, or expert branch maps?

### PIXC, QC, and WSE

9. Which Version D classes may define **extent**? In particular, should dark water and classes 6/7 contribute, be reported as uncertainty bands, or be excluded?
10. Should `water_frac` support only area-equivalent width, or also influence bank/interval probability? How should valid values outside `[0,1]` enter uncertainty calculations?
11. Is `channel_extent_candidate` acceptable as an experimental starting profile, or should a Phase 5-specific profile be scientist-authored after Koshi inspection?
12. What is the required branch-WSE vertical reference and tide convention? Should a future product reproduce RiverObs/RiverTile's four-term subtraction, and which FES model must be recorded?
13. Should WSE be reported only for intervals with adequate height-eligible pixels, independently of extent width availability?

### Geometry, uncertainty, and validation

14. What bank definition is scientifically intended for an irregular pixel cloud: classified-pixel edge, area-equivalent edge, hull/mask intersection, probabilistic contour, or an imagery-constrained boundary?
15. What minimum branch width, minimum wet gap, point-gap tolerance, and uncertainty threshold are scientifically meaningful at SWOT PIXC resolution?
16. Who chooses/approves transect orientation, spacing, corridor width, and junction exclusion distance for the first Koshi benchmark?
17. What accuracy/bias/repeatability criteria must be met before labeling any output “research-grade,” separately for branch width, total wet width, and outer span?
18. Should validation optimize one target metric, or present sensitivity without tuning on the same Koshi observations used for evaluation?
19. Is an initial manually reviewed Option A dataset desired as the reference set against which Options B/C would later be judged?

Until these decisions are made, implementing an automatic centerline, branch segmentation, island detector, bank rule, WSE correction, or width scalar would encode unresolved science as software defaults. This review therefore stops at architecture alternatives.
